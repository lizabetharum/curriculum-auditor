## r09 (Task B: Something tall outside)

Our data (we measured the oak tree by the parking lot): distance 15.0 m, angle 38°, eye height 1.5 m.

Step 1. tan(38°) = opposite / adjacent = (tree above my eye) / 15
Step 2. tree above my eye = 15 × tan(38°) = 11.72 m
Step 3. total height = 11.72 + 1.5 = 13.22 m, so about 13.2 m

For the absent classmate:
We couldn't reach the top, but we could measure how far away we stood and how steep we had to look up. Those two things make a right triangle: the ground distance is the side next to the angle, and the part of the tree above your eyes is the side across from the angle. Tangent is the ratio that connects exactly those two sides, which is why we used tan and not sin or cos (we never knew the slanted line from our eye to the top).
You might ask why we add 1.5 at the end. The angle starts at your eye, not at the ground, so the triangle only reaches from eye level up. The last 1.5 m is the bit under your eyes.
You might also ask if 13.2 m is reasonable. My warm-up guess was 12 m, and the tree is about as tall as the four-story building next to it (about 3 m per story), so yes.

Assumptions:
1. The ground is flat between us and the tree. If the ground goes uphill toward the tree, the base is higher than our feet, so our answer is too high. To reduce it: measure the angle down to the base too and subtract that part.
2. The tree is straight up and down. If it leans toward us, the top is closer than the base and our answer is too high. Leaning away makes it too low. To reduce it: measure from two sides of the tree and average.
3. The top we sighted is directly above the base. With a wide crown you might aim at a branch that sticks out toward you, which makes the answer too high. To reduce it: walk around until the highest point lines up with the trunk.
4. The clinometer is accurate. At 38°, being off by 1° changes the height by about 0.4 m. To reduce it: take three readings each and average them.
